PHD / CURRENT RESEARCH

Graphene, proximity effects & two-dimensional materials.

Investigating interfacial electronic interactions using first-principles calculations in graphene-based systems.

SCIENTIFIC MOTIVATION

From interfaces to electronic bands

Graphene has a distinctive low-energy electronic structure. Proximity to another atomically thin material may introduce exchange and spin–orbit interactions that are weak or absent in isolated graphene.

My doctoral direction explores graphene coupled to V-doped WSe₂. Questions of interest include how magnetic proximity affects graphene electronic bands, what role interfacial spin–orbit coupling plays, and how valley-resolved behavior connects to possible topological states.

The quantum anomalous Hall effect is a research motivation, not a verified result of this ongoing project. Public data and conclusions will be added only after advisor approval and, where applicable, publication or preprint release.

01

Materials question

How can electronic states in graphene respond to a magnetic and spin–orbit-active neighboring material?

02

Computational approach

Density functional theory, interface modeling, electronic-band interpretation, and convergence testing.

03

Analysis focus

Exchange-related features, SOC-related features, low-energy dispersion near the K and K′ valleys.

04

Current status

Research in progress. Calculated figures, parameters, spin textures, and claims are not publicly posted here.
