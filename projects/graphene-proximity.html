Graphene for

Investigating electronic interactions in graphene-based heterostructures using first-principles calculations.

SCIENTIFIC MOTIVATION

A neighboring material
can change the question.

My doctoral direction explores graphene coupled to V-doped WSe₂.

The research asks how magnetic proximity and interfacial spin–orbit interactions may affect graphene’s low-energy bands and valley-resolved behavior.

Density functional theory, interface modeling, convergence checks, and electronic-structure interpretation provide the computational foundation.

Possible topological electronic states and the quantum anomalous Hall effect motivate this direction; they are not established outcomes of this project.

HOW THE CURRENT PROJECT WORKS

From an interface
to an electronic response.

01

Model the layers

Represent graphene beside V-doped WSe₂ and examine the interface geometry. The interactive display illustrates layer arrangement.

02

Run first-principles calculations

Use DFT tools, including QuantumATK and OpenMX, with HPC resources and Slurm to investigate electronic structure.

03

Examine the bands

Investigate how magnetic proximity and spin–orbit interactions influence low-energy and valley-resolved electronic behavior.

04

Interpret device relevance

Assess mechanisms that could inform electronic-material design. Possible topological behavior remains a research question.

The 2D/3D display explains geometry. It is not a DFT calculation, validated atomic structure, or demonstration of the quantum anomalous Hall effect.
