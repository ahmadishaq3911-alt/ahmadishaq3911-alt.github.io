SCIENTIFIC MOTIVATION

What happens when two
atomic layers meet?

Graphene is exceptionally thin and conductive. Putting it next to a magnetic 2D material may change the way its electrons behave.

My doctoral research uses density functional theory (DFT) to examine magnetic proximity, spin–orbit coupling, and changes in electronic structure.

The long-term motivation is to understand pathways toward energy-efficient electronics and potentially unidirectional topological edge transport. These are possibilities under investigation, not demonstrated outcomes.

THE RESEARCH WORKFLOW

From an interface to electronic behavior.

01

Build the interface

Model graphene adjacent to a magnetic two-dimensional material and examine interlayer geometry.

02

Check the calculation

Use DFT and high-performance computing while testing numerical convergence and model sensitivity.

03

Analyze electronic states

Investigate low-energy bands, magnetic proximity, spin–orbit effects, and valley-related behavior.

04

Evaluate the physics

Explore which electronic features might eventually support low-power or topological applications.

CURRENT STATUS

Research in progress.

The work focuses on understanding how graphene responds to neighboring magnetic materials. Electronic-structure calculations guide the next questions.

Berry curvature, topological invariants, and edge-state physics are relevant future or ongoing investigations. The quantum anomalous Hall effect and dissipationless current are research motivations—not established results of this project.
