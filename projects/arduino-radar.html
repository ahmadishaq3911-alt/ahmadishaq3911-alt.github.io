THE PROBLEM

Where is the obstacle?

Detect a nearby object, estimate its distance, and show its direction with inexpensive components.

I integrated an ultrasonic sensor with a rotating servo, controlled the hardware with Arduino, and used Processing to display the scan.

The project uses sound-based ranging with a radar-style display. It is an ultrasonic scanner, rather than a radio-frequency radar.

What I used

  • Arduino UNO microcontroller
  • HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor
  • SG90 servo motor
  • Jumper wires and USB connection
  • Arduino IDE, Processing, and Tera Term

HOW IT WORKS

Scan. Measure. Display.

01

Rotate

The servo moves the sensor through an approximately 180° sweep.

02

Measure

The sensor sends a sound pulse. Echo travel time gives the object’s distance.

03

Send

Arduino sends distance and servo-angle data through the serial connection.

04

Display

Processing places the readings on a radar-style screen.

TESTING & RESULTS

Check the readings against a ruler.

The two reported test points produced a mean absolute percentage error of approximately 6%.

TestRuler distanceScanner readingAbsolute error
18.6 cm8.0 cm0.6 cm · 7.0%
224.2 cm23.0 cm1.2 cm · 5.0%

These measurements come from the supplied project report. They describe two short-range tests, not a validated accuracy rating over the full sensor range.

What I demonstrated

Sensor integration, servo control, serial communication, graphical data display, and comparison against reference measurements.

What I would improve

Test more distances, angles, and target materials; calibrate systematic error and assess repeatability.
